Plan of proof. After fixing conventions, we recall the coherent-state setup and the Dorau-Much
flux identity. We then differentiate the horizon flux at s = 0 and estimate the variation of the
null energy density in terms of g and a local energy norm of the coherent profile. Auxiliary
geometric and causal-support statements are recorded to isolate horizon contributions and to
connect with local Rindler bookkeeping used elsewhere in the literature.

Organisation. Section 3 fixes conventions and basic definitions. Section 4 records the curvature
remainder estimate for the flat boost current in Riemann normal coordinates. Section 5 proves
an exact cap-elimination lemma for compactly supported coherent profiles. Section 6 recalls the
relative Cauchy evolution and its stress-tensor implementation in the free Klein-Gordon theory.
Section 7 contains the proof of Theorem 1. A brief discussion of the additional modular input
needed for “local Rindler” generalisations appears in Section 8.

8 Beyond exact horizon symmetry

The argument proving Theorem 1 relies on an exact geometric identification of modular structure
with the y-flow, encoded in the Dorau-Much horizon-flux identity [DM25]. In settings where
one only has an approzimate local boost symmetry (for instance, a small normal neighborhood
in a generic curved spacetime), two additional issues appear.

First, Stokes’ theorem applied to a boost current J* = Ty, over a “null-box” region €
produces contributions from all boundary components. The causal-support lemma of Section 5
provides one mechanism to eliminate the cap terms for compactly supported coherent profiles.
Independently, Section 4 shows that the bulk defect V,.J* is controlled by curvature through the
explicit bound

Second, and more fundamentally, relating such geometric flux expressions to relative entropy
requires quantitative control of modular objects when the modular flow is no longer geometric.
